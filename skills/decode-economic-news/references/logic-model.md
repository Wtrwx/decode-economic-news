# Causal Logic Model

Use this model to turn an event into a testable explanation.

## 1. Form the contradiction

Express the puzzle as two facts that appear inconsistent:

```text
Actor X should prefer A because of condition B, but chose C. Why?
Metric X looks strong, while related metric Y weakened. What changed?
```

Avoid emotional labels at this stage.

## 2. Build the actor matrix

For every important actor record:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Goal | What outcome is optimized? |
| Constraint | What cannot be changed cheaply? |
| Resource | What can the actor deploy? |
| Time horizon | Days, quarters, or years? |
| Outside option | What happens without the decision? |
| Observable evidence | Which action or document supports this? |

Do not infer private intent solely from who benefits.

## 3. Separate causes

- Trigger: the event that changed timing.
- Enabling condition: what made the outcome possible.
- Structural cause: the persistent incentive or constraint.
- Amplifier: what increased magnitude.
- Narrative: how participants describe the event.

Prefer a layered explanation over one totalizing cause.

## 4. Write the transmission chain

Use explicit links:

```text
shock -> input cost/constraint -> actor decision -> market or policy response
      -> first-order result -> second-order result -> feedback loop
```

Attach a fact ID or a clearly marked inference to every link. If a link lacks evidence, list the observation that would confirm or falsify it.

## 5. Test alternatives

Use at least one:

- Time comparison: did the relationship hold before the event?
- Cross-sectional comparison: did peers without the proposed cause behave differently?
- Counterfactual: what should be observed if the competing explanation were true?
- Scale calculation: is the proposed mechanism large enough to explain the outcome?
- Actor inconsistency: does another action contradict the stated motive?

## 6. Conclude conditionally

State:

1. Best-supported explanation.
2. Evidence strength.
3. Important alternative.
4. Next observable signal.
5. Condition that would invalidate the conclusion.

## 7. Extend the model to prediction and stock selection

Do not start from the highest quantitative score. Start from the expectation gap:

```text
observable contradiction
-> market's surface explanation
-> actor incentive and hard constraint
-> structural variable that can persist
-> dated catalyst
-> transmission to order/revenue/profit/cash flow
-> valuation or positioning response
-> second-order beneficiary/loser
-> falsification signal
```

Use quantitative rankings to decide where to investigate. Promote a candidate into a thesis only after resolving its role in the chain, the size and timing of the mechanism, a competing explanation and a falsification signal. A company can rank highly because of price momentum while remaining absent from the causal mechanism.

## 8. Use overseas peers as a natural comparison

For an A-share question, compare the mapped U.S. and Korean sectors after removing their local broad-market move. Ask whether the same operational variable exists in all three markets. Treat an A-share rejection of a positive overseas shock, or resilience to a negative shock, as the contradiction to explain. Test local policy, valuation, positioning and supply-chain differences before concluding that the foreign move will transmit.
